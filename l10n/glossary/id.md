# Indonesian (id) glossary

**Status: draft.** Written for Tally 1.4.2 without a native speaker; it awaits a native speaker's review (the
notes at the end list what to check first).

## Tone and address

Tally speaks to the user as **“kamu”** (with the short forms *-mu*, *padamu*): friendly and personal, the way modern
Indonesian everyday apps do. “Anda” would sound like a bank letter, and “lo/gue” is too slangy for a wide audience.
Sentences stay short and plain; formal or accounting words (*liabilitas*, *dihapusbukukan*) are avoided. The phone is
*ponsel* (neutral and widely understood, more polished than *HP*). A person in a loan is *dia* (gender-neutral).

## For reviewers

- **Written off → *diikhlaskan*** (“let go with a clear heart”) is everyday Indonesian for giving up on money lent;
  **forgiven → *dibebaskan*** (the lender freed you from the debt). Check both read naturally as statuses.
- **Liabilities tab → *Utang***, with the loans section inside it called *Pinjaman*, so the tab and section don’t
  share one word. *Kewajiban* or *Liabilitas* felt too formal.
- **Home buttons *Pinjam* / *Pinjamkan*** (borrow / lend) differ only by the suffix; check they’re clear enough at a
  glance.
- **Good afternoon → *Selamat siang***: the app shows it from 12:00 to 18:00, which covers both *siang* and *sore*.
- **Groceries → *Dapur*** (as in *uang dapur*), next to Shopping → *Belanja*; **Eating out → *Jajan*** (as in
  *uang jajan*). Short enough for the category tiles on small phones at large text; *Belanja dapur* and *Makan di luar*
  were cut.
- **Teal → *Toska***, pink → *Merah jambu*, light red → *Merah terang* (not *merah muda*, which means pink).
- **Calculator “Decimal point” → *Tanda desimal***: Indonesian writes a decimal comma, so “titik” would be wrong.
- **Short store description** says *Pengeluaran sekali ketuk* (“one-tap spending”) to fit 80 characters.
